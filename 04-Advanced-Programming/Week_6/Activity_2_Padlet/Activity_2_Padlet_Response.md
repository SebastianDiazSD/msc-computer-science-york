Different visualisation types serve different analytical purposes. Here is a breakdown of the main types, what data they suit, and the message they communicate most effectively:

| Chart Type | Best Data Type | Message Communicated | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bar chart** | Categorical / discrete numerical | Comparison between categories | Sales per store per month |
| **Grouped bar chart** | Categorical with sub-groups | Comparison within and between categories | Year 1 vs Year 2 grades by subject |
| **Stacked bar chart** | Categorical, parts of a whole | Composition and total magnitude | Grade distribution (F/P/M/D) per subject |
| **Line graph** | Continuous / time series | Trend over time | Baby name popularity 1880–2010 |
| **Histogram** | Continuous numerical | Frequency distribution | Income distribution in a population |
| **Scatter plot** | Two continuous variables | Relationship / correlation | Height vs weight |
| **Box plot** | Continuous, grouped | Distribution, spread, outliers | Salary by department |
| **Heatmap** | Two categorical axes + numerical value | Intensity / correlation matrix | Correlation between nutrient values |
| **Pie chart** | Categorical, parts of a whole (≤6) | Proportional composition | Market share |
| **Pair plot** | Multiple continuous variables | All pairwise relationships at once | EDA on multi-feature datasets |

**Key principle**: the chart type should be determined by the *data type* and *what question you are trying to answer*, not by visual preference. A bar chart makes category comparisons obvious; a pie chart shows proportions but becomes unreadable with more than 5–6 categories; a line graph implies continuity so should only be used when the x-axis represents a genuine continuum (time, temperature, distance).

**For this week's college grades data**, a grouped bar chart is the most appropriate choice for comparing Year 1 vs Year 2 performance across subjects — it shows both the magnitude and the relative composition of grade bands simultaneously, and allows easy comparison between groups.
